What to use

run-comfyui
# Optional: faster repeats with one model family
run-comfyui --cached
# Stop, including cached mode:
stop-comfyui

The normal launcher retains caching disabled. The opt-in --cache-ram 16 sets a RAM-pressure headroom threshold, not a 16 GiB cache-size limit. Caching retained H3 weights while loading Wan and exhausted the eight-GiB reserve; the supervisor stopped that run. Therefore cached mode is explicit: stop and restart before switching model families. A fresh cached Wan server passed both runs with at least 69.5 GiB available and zero sampled runtime-process swap. The original uncached H3→Wan sequence passed with at least 51.6 GiB available.

Use the short 512×288 fixtures for prompt previews, then the full 480p preset. H3 is the regular video/audio choice; Wan AIO is the retained less-restricted silent-video choice. No 720p/1080p performance or unrestricted-content behavior was qualified in this follow-up.

Attention optimization

Leave the extra H3 layout node disabled. The complete matched 480p run took 444.7s (7m 25s), versus 434.2s (7m 14s) without it: about 2.4% longer, with no demonstrated speedup. Sampling-node time was 367.2s versus 354.7s. All 400 block calls used packed QKV at T=14867, so this was an active test, not a fallback. The ready H3 graph remains unchanged.

The isolated forced-flash BF16 B1/H56/T14500/D128 component test showed zero absolute error and 1,063ms → 402ms (2.65×), but bypassed ComfyUI’s wrapper. The installed comfy/ops.py already makes strided K/V contiguous on gfx1151 when they exceed its L2 threshold. This likely explains the lack of an additional whole-video gain. The full encoded videos were visually similar but not bit-identical (decoded-video PSNR 44.20dB); decoded audio matched exactly. These single-run results do not establish a general regression or bit-exact equivalence. The small pinned node package remains available for explicit experiments; it applies no global patch.

Would another engine help?

Keep ComfyUI / ROCm as the practical default. It already supports these installed quantized packs and the browser workflows. h3-hip.c is a real gfx1151-native candidate, but needs roughly 134 GiB of original FL2VA weights. Its faster presets change step counts, use approximate attention or a softer tiny decoder; their published timings do not establish a speedup for our eight-step workflow. It was researched, not installed or benchmarked here.

Gufo’s H3 report still has no qualified complete-video timing matrix, and its BF16 checkpoint path differs from our Comfy INT8 pack. Its LLM prefill rate does not predict video-generation speed.

For Wan, FastVideo has ROCm paths, but does not provide a complete device/model validation matrix. No faster gfx1151 result or compatibility with this custom AIO pack was established here. A generic engine change is therefore a research candidate, not a measured improvement.

What was checked

Exact output dimensions and frame counts, video decoding, H3 audio-stream presence, sample frames, per-node wall times, available unified memory, process swap, power and temperatures.
